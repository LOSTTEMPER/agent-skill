## 1. Industry-system scan

Use only the dimensions that can change the decision.

### Macro conditions

- regulation and public policy;
- economic incentives and capital availability;
- technology and infrastructure maturity;
- cultural or behavioral change;
- supply constraints and channel access;
- standards, interoperability, and data rights.

### Industry structure

- existing competitors and intensity;
- substitutes and internal workarounds;
- supplier power and concentration;
- buyer power and procurement logic;
- entry barriers and new entrants;
- complementors and ecosystem dependencies.

### Value-flow map

For every actor, record:

| Actor | Gives | Receives | Bears cost | Bears risk | Has veto | Switching condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

A model is fragile when value accrues to one party while recurring work, risk, or cost is silently shifted to another.

## 2. Competitor selection and matrix

Select competitors by the analysis purpose:

- **Direct**: same customer, need, and mechanism.
- **Substitute**: same or similar need, different mechanism.
- **Budget or attention competitor**: consumes the same scarce resource.
- **Reference**: not a competitor, but demonstrates a useful mechanism.

Suggested matrix:

Do not compare features with different scopes, tiers, user roles, or versions as though they are equivalent.

## 3. Positioning test

Complete:

```text
For [specific segment]
who struggle with [important problem in a defined context],
the product is [category or frame]
that delivers [measurable or observable value]
through [distinct mechanism].
Unlike [primary alternative],
it wins because [credible capability or tradeoff].
```

Test:

- Is the segment narrow enough to guide choices?
- Is the problem costly or frequent enough?
- Is the value meaningful and provable?
- Is the mechanism distinctive and feasible?
- Does the position state what the product will not optimize?
- Can the organization deliver the promise repeatedly?

## 4. Business-model test

### Customer and value

- Who uses, decides, pays, operates, and bears risk?
- What current alternative is displaced?
- What outcome justifies switching?
- What proof is needed before purchase?

### Economics

- What is the price metric?
- What drives acquisition, service, and support cost?
- What is the contribution margin at the intended scale?
- When is cash received relative to delivery cost?
- Which costs grow faster than revenue?
- What happens under optimistic, base, and pessimistic adoption?

### Operations

- What recurring human work is required?
- Which dependency can block delivery?
- Which service level is promised?
- How are exceptions, disputes, and failures handled?

### Sustainability

- Does each essential participant have a reason to stay?
- Are incentives aligned with customer outcomes?
- Does the model rely on subsidies, lock-in, opacity, or regulatory gaps?
- What makes it hard to copy: compounding data with rights, workflow integration, trust, distribution, scale, brand, network liquidity, know-how, or cost structure?

## 5. Differentiation and moat

Differentiate on a mechanism that customers value, not decorative feature variance.

Possible mechanisms:

- lower total cost or risk;
- faster time to value;
- superior outcome or reliability;
- easier workflow and adoption;
- specialized context or segment fit;
- trusted distribution or authorization;
- integrated complements;
- unique supply or operational capability.

Classify defensibility:

- temporary feature lead;
- execution and learning speed;
- workflow and integration depth;
- data advantage with legitimate rights;
- brand and trust;
- distribution;
- scale or cost advantage;
- network effects with demonstrated liquidity;
- regulation, certification, or scarce assets.

State what would erode the moat.
